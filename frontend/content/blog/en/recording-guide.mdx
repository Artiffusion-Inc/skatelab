## Choose the question first

Before recording, agree with the coach what you want to see. The approach to a jump, takeoff and exit all need context, but may not need the same camera position. Do not try to capture a universal record of every mistake. One clear question tells you what must stay visible.

This is guidance for preparing material, not instructions for performing an element. Technique, training load and safe organisation remain the coach’s responsibility. Recording an attempt does not automatically make it suitable for precise measurement.

## Keep the beginning and what follows

Start before the element and stop after the exit. Do not trim a clip to the jump alone: the coach may need the approach in context, direction of travel and preparation for takeoff. After ice contact, the continuation matters as well as the first landing frame.

Keep the entire skater in view. Arms, head and both skates can matter to the question. Leave space at the edges so a hand or blade does not disappear during a change of direction. Avoid digital zoom during the element: it changes image scale and quality while the movement is happening.

## Find a safe position

Agree the recording position with the coach and rink rules. Do not step into another skater’s path for a better angle. A tripod must not block access, obscure sightlines or create a collision risk. The person filming also needs to stay aware of the surroundings.

Where possible, keep a similar angle for attempts you intend to compare. If you must follow the skater, pan smoothly and retain a wide enough frame. Side and diagonal views can make the same element look very different; that is not necessarily a change in technique.

## Check a short clip at the rink

Open a test recording before the main series. Can you distinguish the skates? Does bright window light hide the skater? Do the boards obscure the lower body? If movement is blurred, investigate camera settings and lighting before continuing.

A high frame rate alone does not guarantee quality. In poor light, individual frames can still be blurred or noisy. Do not use software-generated intermediate frames for analysis: those images were not captured by the camera. They should not be presented as observations.

## Keep the original and its context

Retain the original camera file. Messenger sharing, editing and repeated export can alter resolution, frame rate or timestamps. You can make a separate discussion copy, but do not replace the original.

A short note can identify the element, angle and coach’s question. Avoid unnecessary personal details. Agree who has access, where the file is stored and when it will be deleted. For children’s recordings, discuss the required permissions with responsible adults and the organisation beforehand. General website advice is not a substitute for that process.

## Before the next attempt

Watch the complete recording, then select the relevant [movement phase](/how-it-works#phases). If an important moment is hidden or blurred, record that limitation and film again instead of making a confident conclusion from a missing detail. [Sensor data](/blog/en/video-and-sensors) may add another perspective, but cannot automatically restore everything the video misses.
